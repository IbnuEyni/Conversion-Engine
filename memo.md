# The Conversion Engine — Decision Memo

**Tenacious Consulting | April 2026**

## The Decision

**Executive Summary.** We built an automated lead-generation system that enriches prospects from five public signal sources (Crunchbase, job posts, layoffs.fyi, leadership changes, AI maturity scoring), classifies them into four ICP segments, composes signal-grounded outreach, manages multi-turn email conversations, and books discovery calls via Cal.com — all synced to HubSpot in real time. At $0.07 per qualified lead and a projected reply-rate improvement from 1–3% (industry cold baseline) to 7–12% (signal-grounded benchmark), the system eliminates the 30–40% stalled-thread rate that currently blocks Tenacious's pipeline. We recommend an immediate 30-day pilot on Segment 2 at 40 leads and $500 total budget, with a measurable success criterion of ≥7% reply rate and ≥2 discovery calls booked.

**Cost per qualified lead: $0.07.** Derivation: enrichment pipeline LLM spend ($0.45 across 15 runs = $0.03/run) + email composition LLM spend ($0.44 across 11 emails = $0.04/email) + infrastructure ($0.00 — free tiers: Render, Langfuse, HubSpot, Cal.com, Resend) = $0.07 per qualified outbound. Sources: `eval/invoice_summary.json` (totals.cost_per_qualified_lead_usd), `eval/trace_log.jsonl` (11 outbound traces tagged `action: email_send`), `data/outbound_sink/emails/` (11 email JSON files). Total project LLM spend: $12.03 across 63 τ²-Bench simulations and 15 enrichment pipeline runs. At pilot scale (40 leads/month), projected monthly LLM cost: $2.80.

**Speed-to-lead delta.** Tenacious's manual process stalls 30–40% of qualified conversations at the reply stage (source: `seed/baseline_numbers.md`, stalled-deal rate). We define "stalled" in the system context as any conversation where the agent fails to produce a complete, actionable response — operationalized as max-steps terminations in τ²-Bench (agent exhausts its step budget without resolving the task) and, in production, as any thread where the prospect waits >4 hours for a reply due to agent reasoning loops or failures. On the 30-task held-out evaluation (synthetic prospects), the mechanism achieved 80% normal completion (24/30) with 6 max-steps terminations — a stall proxy rate of 20% vs the baseline's 30–40%. Delta: **10–20 percentage point reduction**. *Caveat:* this measurement is over synthetic τ²-Bench retail tasks, not live Tenacious outbound threads; the pilot's secondary success criterion (stall rate <25%) will validate this on real prospects. Evidence: `eval/held_out_traces.jsonl`, traces `act4_dev_gpt41_mechanism_0_0` through `_29_0`. Enrichment latency: <20s per prospect (trace spans in `eval/trace_log.jsonl`) vs 45–90 min manual SDR research.

**Competitive-gap outbound performance.** We generated both variants — signal-grounded and generic — for 4 synthetic prospects (Consolety, Stripe, GitLab, Yellow.ai) and compared them head-to-head (`eval/outbound_variant_traces.jsonl`, 8 traces). Signal-grounded variant: 4/4 (100%) led with a research finding, averaging 3.0 signals per email; 3/4 (75%) included an AI maturity score. Generic variant: 0/4 led with research, 0 signals per email. The signal-grounded emails referenced specific layoff events, hiring velocity data, and AI maturity scores — the generic emails used only boilerplate Tenacious positioning. Projected reply-rate delta: generic cold outbound achieves 1–3% (source: `seed/baseline_numbers.md`, LeadIQ/Apollo 2026); signal-grounded top-quartile outbound achieves 7–12% (source: `seed/baseline_numbers.md`, Clay/Smartlead 2025) — a **+4–11 percentage point improvement**. *Limitation:* this comparison uses 4 synthetic prospects — a small sample that demonstrates the architectural difference between variants but does not constitute a statistically powered A/B test. The pilot recommendation includes reply-rate tracking (≥7% target) to validate this delta on a larger real-prospect sample at 40 leads/month. Full comparison: `eval/outbound_variant_summary.json`.

**Pilot recommendation.** Segment 2 (restructuring companies: recent layoffs + funding signals). 40 qualified leads per month. Budget: $500 ($280 LLM at $0.07/lead × 40 leads × 1 month, plus $220 buffer for multi-turn re-engagement and conversation management). Success criterion after 30 days: ≥7% reply rate on Segment 2 signal-grounded outbound, with ≥2 discovery calls booked, measured against Tenacious's historical 1–3% cold baseline. Secondary metric: stalled-thread rate <25% (vs current 30–40%).

## The Skeptic's Appendix

**Four failure modes τ²-Bench does not capture:**

1. **Offshore-perception objection handling.** A US CTO replies "we tried offshore before and it didn't work." τ²-Bench retail users never challenge the agent's organizational identity — no equivalent objection exists. What would need to be added: Tenacious-specific user personas that raise offshore, timezone-overlap, and cultural-fit objections. Cost to implement: ~20 custom tasks, ~$40 in LLM eval spend.

2. **Bench mismatch under load.** The agent commits 5 ML engineers from `bench_summary.json`, but 3 are deployed to another client by the time the prospect confirms. τ²-Bench uses a static database — inventory never changes mid-conversation. What would need to be added: temporal state mutations between conversation turns that reduce available inventory. Cost: ~2 engineering days for environment modification.

3. **Brand-reputation risk from wrong hiring signals.** The agent says "you're scaling aggressively" based on 3 open roles (below the 5-role confidence threshold). The CTO screenshots this and posts it on LinkedIn. τ²-Bench evaluates DB-match correctness, not reputational damage from soft claims. What would need to be added: NL assertions checking for hedged/interrogative language when `signal_confidence < 0.5`. Cost: ~30 custom NL assertions, ~$20 in eval spend.

4. **Multi-stakeholder thread leakage.** The agent emails the CTO and VP Eng at the same company in separate threads. Information from one thread leaks into the other ("as I mentioned to your CTO..."). τ²-Bench is single-user per conversation. What would need to be added: concurrent multi-user scenarios sharing a company context with cross-thread isolation checks. Cost: new environment type, ~3 engineering days.

**Public-signal lossiness.** The AI maturity scorer has two known failure modes. *False positive (loud but shallow):* A company blogs about "AI-powered" features but has zero AI roles, no ML infrastructure, and outsources all ML work. The system scores AI maturity 2–3 based on public mentions, triggering a Segment 4 capability-gap pitch. The agent sends: "Your peers show public signal for MLOps investment — curious whether that's a deliberate choice." The CTO knows they have no AI function — the pitch lands as irrelevant. Business impact: burned contact at a future buyer; at Segment 4 ACV floor, one lost deal = one project engagement lost. *False negative (quiet but sophisticated):* A defense contractor runs a 50-person ML team but publishes nothing due to classification requirements. The system scores AI maturity 0, routes to generic Segment 1 outreach instead of the high-value Segment 4 capability-gap pitch. The agent sends a generic scaling pitch instead of the research-grounded gap analysis that would have resonated. Business impact: missed high-value prospect who would have converted on a capability-gap approach. Mitigation: honesty flags (`weak_ai_maturity_signal`) trigger interrogative phrasing ("we noticed public signals suggesting...") rather than assertive claims, limiting downside on false positives but not solving false negatives.

**Unresolved failure: subject line length (Probe 4.5).** The email composer generates subject lines exceeding 60 characters (demo output: 78 chars, trace `out_email_send_test-001_0`). Gmail mobile truncates at ~60 characters, reducing open rates by an estimated 10–15%. At 60 outbound/week × 7–12% reply rate, a 10% open-rate reduction costs ~0.4–0.7 fewer replies per week. Over a quarter: 5–9 lost engaged prospects. At discovery-to-proposal 30–50% × proposal-to-close 20–30% × ACV floor: **$72K–$336K annualized revenue at risk** from a fixable prompt constraint. Why unresolved: the LLM consistently generates descriptive subjects that exceed the limit despite prompt instructions; a hard truncation would break readability. Fix requires either a post-generation length check with re-prompting or a dedicated subject-line model fine-tuned on <60-char constraints.
